# Decisions — site-model-2 (builders' requests on `src/data/site.js`)

Format: **choice** — why (source / reasoning) — alternative. ⚙ = reconstruction-class. The disposition of each request is in
`docs/site-model.md` §10. No element id changed. Added: element `context.kiponus-approach`; zones `lishkat-pinchas` and
`lishkat-chavitin`, with ZONE_CONTENT entries.

## Har HaBayit and the context
- **Stoa overhangs are recorded as `params.overhangs`, not folded into the boxes; STOA_ROOF stays 20.** — The builder's front row stands on the band's inner edge, so its entablature and cornice reach 2 amot beyond the band. The parapet rises to +21.67. The raised gate bays reach +23.6 (Kiponus, Tadi) and +21.95 plus a parapet (Shushan). Each overhang has a box and `solidFromZ`, and `parahSightLine()` counts the east stoa's as obstacles; they cost the line nothing. — Alternative: STOA_ROOF ≥ 22. No source sets the stoa height (§20 #7). It would also break the builder's raised bays, and put the east roof level with the low east wall.
- **The Kiponus approach is a level deck at Z0 from the gate west to where the west slope regains 740 m (235.5 m), 10 clear between 1-amah parapets** ⚙. — Middot 1:3: the gate "serves entry and exit". It stands about 18 m above the Tyropoeon at the wall's foot, so an approach is implied, and a level deck is the modest reading. — Alternative: a stair down into the valley. That would be a second unsourced form, and the builder already built the deck.
- **The Kiponus deck is in zone `outside` (Jerusalem, rung 2), not in `gate-kiponus` (rung 3)** (review fix). — The deck lies west of the Mount wall, over the Tyropoeon, in the city. The Chuldah passages are no precedent: they run under the Mount, and their zones carry Jerusalem's rung on the strength of Rambam BhB 6:9. The `gate-kiponus` content is Har HaBayit's ("a gate of Har HaBayit, with its rung"). `outside` is already "Jerusalem, around the Temple Mount" at rung 2, and the mount builder's `mount.gate.kiponus.bridge` anchor already declares it. — Alternative: a new `kiponus-approach` zone at rung 2. That would give an unsourced structure a named place, and the UI and the anchor would have to change.
- **Chuldah stairs: whole risers. Each rise is the drop ÷ N (0.5015 at 48 cm), treads stay ½.** — The drop is the real Ophel–platform difference (13 m), so it cannot be a whole number of half-amot under every amah standard. Middot 2:3's ½ is kept within 1%. — Alternative: exact ½ rises with a 4 cm fall along the level passage. That changes the passage floor the builder built.
- **The Parah kohen stands on the arrangement, 1 amah in from its east edge, east of the heifer; `kohenEye` +153.0** (48 cm) ⚙ place on the top. — Parah 3:9: the heifer is laid "on the arrangement", the kohen "stands in the east, facing west" and wipes his hand on her body, then "descended (ירד) and kindled". The pyre (8 high ⚙) is under his feet, not between him and the Heichal. A viewer on the crest with no pyre is kept as `groundEye`, and the line passes from both (17.17 / 17.02 amot). Consumers that used `kohenEye` as a ground point (the `mount.har-hamishchah` anchor in mount/posts.js, the terrain/plan.js viewpoint) now land inside the pyre footprint and must read `groundEye`; that switch has to land in the same commit (review). — Alternatives: lower the pyre under a ground eye (contradicts ירד); or move the pyre off the axis (the Mishnah has him sprinkle over her toward the KhK).
- **The heifer's envelope on the top is 5 (N–S) × 2 × 2** ⚙, head south (Parah 3:9). — Needed to show that the kohen's line clears her: by 1.17 amot. — Alternative: none (unsourced size).
- **§4's robustness line was corrected.** "650–900 m: 16.6–17.4; landing 8.75" did not match the function. Beyond the crest the ridge itself blocks, and the causeway landing sees 3.99 (the Shushan HaBirah chamber takes the low lines). The committed site.js gives the same numbers, so the correction is to the text, not to this pass's geometry.

## Courts and chambers
- **Middot 2:3's "all the openings and gates there, 20 high and 10 wide" covers the gates and the great doorways, not chamber doors.** — Zevachim 55b quotes the rule as "all the GATES there, 20 high" against the 8-amah pishpeshin of the beit hachalifot, and answers "pishpeshin are different" (פשפשין שאני); the lul behind the Kaporet is 8 too. Rambam BhB 5:5 gives the measure for "every gate of them" (כל שער מהן). Middot 4:3 gives each ta three openings, which cannot be 20 high in rooms 5–7 high. And the Moked has its "small pishpash" (Middot 1:7). These are now in `CHAMBER_DOOR_H`'s comment and `azarah.chamber.gazit.params.doorRule` (review). — Alternative: raise the six chambers' roofs to ≥ 22 for 20-amah doors. Not adopted. The KG's four kodesh immersions were in the mikveh on the Parvah roof (Middot 5:3; Yoma 31a). Abaye derives the En-Eitam head (+23) from the Water Gate's 20 + 3, which a higher Parvah roof would exceed. And Middot 5:4 keeps the north trio's roofs level.
- **The six chambers' doors are 16 high** (`CHAMBER_DOOR_H` ⚙ = the 20-amah roof less a 1-amah slab and a 3-amah lintel course), widths as built (10, the Gazit east door 8, the Medichin 6). The Golah's is 4 × 8; Pinchas and the chavitin chamber get 4 × 8 doors to Ezrat Yisrael. — These were recorded from what build-courts built, so nothing moves. — Alternative: the pishpash scale (8) for all; it looks too small on the 45-amah Gazit, which held the lotteries.
- **The Gazit east door is the clear opening Y 372–380, with 1-amah piers at the chamber's SE corner and before the Golah's SW corner.** — The old Y 371–381 used the whole free stretch of the east face, leaving no jamb at either end, and its door-case would run into the Golah. — Alternative: 9 wide (372–381), flush with the Golah corner. The builder's door-case then collides.
- **The Azarah's N and S walls run through the east corner squares to X 260; the low E wall spans between them (Y 263–398).** — This is the convention the EN walls already follow at their own east corners. The corner rises to the high wall's 56 and steps to the EN's 46 at X 260, with no notch. — Alternative: raise the E wall's corner squares to 56 as separate pieces. That needs a new kind of piece, for the same geometry.
- **Water Gate roof: stair, mikveh and walk recorded as built.** The newel stair is r 1.95 in the east room. The mikveh tank is 3½ × 2½, its water 2.7 × 1.7 × 3, still ≥ the 1 × 1 × 3 of Yoma 31a. The walk along the court edge runs Y 261–262.6. — A 3-amah stair does not pass a person, and the full 4 × 4 tank closed the walk to Beit Avtinas. — Alternative: move Beit Avtinas. That needs a smaller Avtinas or a wider gatehouse, and both are unsourced.
- **The roof east of the mikveh stays unwalkable (request rejected).** — It is the stair's open well. From there the court's S wall, 2.6 amot away and 20 high, blocks 50°–130°, against 85°–115° from the existing walk. The walk's east end already sees 130°–160° clear of the built structures. Yoma 28b has one man "on a roof" (הך דקאי אאיגרא) and one on the ground, but does not say which roof. — Alternatives for the schedule: the east stoa roof (35°–175° clear over the low east wall), or the walk's east end.
- **The Barkai watcher faces the eastern sky, not Hebron** (review fix to the schedule hand-off). — "The whole face of the east is lit, as far as Hebron" (Yoma 3:1; Tamid 3:2; Yoma 28b) says how far the eastern light has spread. Hebron bears ≈203° (SSW, 30.5 km) from the Mikdash, so "south-east toward Hebron" was a wrong heading. The hand-off now asks for a view of the eastern sky, about 90°–135°. — Alternative: face Hebron (≈203°). Nothing in the texts has the watcher look there.
- **Medichin's winding stair: r 1.9 in a 5 × 5 box in the chamber's SW corner, including its walls**, as built. — Middot 5:3 gives the stair; its size is ⚙.
- **The kiyor well is a carve in the court fill, listed in `azarah.floor.params.shafts`.** — The courts are solid from Z0. The well (Yoma 37a–b) belongs to the altar builder's lift but must be cut by courts. — Alternative: a new element. The muchni's `params.well` already fixes it.

## The altar
- **Nanasin: shaft 3 + cedar block 1 = 4 amot** ⚙ (was 5 + 1). — "Dwarf" pillars (Middot 3:5; Tamid 3:5). The three rows of iron hooks "in them" come within a standing kohen's reach (+3.2 to +3.8 ≈ 1.5–1.8 m) for hanging the lamb by its hind leg (Tamid 4:2: "נוקבו מתוך ערכובו ותולה בו"). — Alternative: keep 5 and lead the lower two rows into the shaft (the builder's interim fix). "בהם" allows the pillars themselves, but the pillars are then no longer "dwarf".
- **The reach rule covers the lamb only; the rows' spacing (0.3 amah) is ⚙; the rows stay in the cedar block** (review). — EK II 58 collects the readings. Rash and Bartenura (so too Shiltei HaGiborim; Tavnit Heichal: "each high or low according to its size") put three rows one above another, facing the tables: low for a lamb, middle for a ram, high for a bull, which needs a tall block. Raavyah (in Tosafot Yom Tov) puts one row on three sides (N, E, S). Raavad on Tamid has them under a cedar board laid across two pillars. On the first reading a bull hung from the top row at ≈1.9 m would not clear the pavement, and the sources do not say how the bulls were handled; `heightNote` and `rowsNote` now say so. The rows are not spread into the shaft: EK II 51 gives the reason for the cedar ("in stone the hooks cannot be fixed"), and on every reading the rows are in the cedar. `hookRowsZ` is unchanged (build-altar computes the same 0.2/0.5/0.8 of the block). — Alternative: a taller block (Rash's "thick, i.e. tall") with wider spacing. It still would not lift a bull clear, and it would cost the pillars their dwarf height.
- **The small ramps are slabs of the kevesh's mass, with the underside 1 amah below their low end** ⚙. The sovev branch is carried on the kevesh's east face, open below +21, its south end face 2.47 high. The yesod branch's underside is the pavement, so it stays solid. — Zevachim 62b / Rambam BhB 2:14: "two small ramps go OUT FROM the kevesh". — Alternative: solid to the floor, leaving a 7½-amah blank pier face beside the ramp.
- **The sovev branch's box runs to the pavement again (+16); the carried slab is `params.undersideZ` +21 with a `boxNote`** (review fix). — build-altar sweeps the branch from `box.min[2]` with faces 'esn' (no underside), so a +21 box bottom left the slab open to the sky from below (review shot `sovev-under-up`). The first pass's claim "carried block, no sky visible through it" was wrong: its shot was taken from a shallow angle, where the kevesh's east face hid the hole. With the box to the pavement the branch is built solid and closed until build-altar sweeps it from `undersideZ` with an underside ('esnu'). Like `altar.kevesh`'s box, the box is the overall extent. Verified with two low-tier renders from the east and south-east (`shots/site-model-2-fix/`), and with raycasts: the branch's east face is hit at X 227 at both +18 and +22. — Alternative: keep the box at +21 and rely on build-altar's one-word change landing in the same commit. That would leave a hole if the commits split.

## The Heichal
- **Ner ma'aravi = lamp 1 from the east** (`params.nerMaaravi`), with the Rambam's middle lamp recorded as `readings.northSouth`. — Lock #11 sets an E–W row. Tamid 6:1: "he found the two eastern lamps burning, cleaned the eastern and left the western" — the western of the two easternmost is the second from the east. — Alternative: the middle lamp (Rambam BhB 3:8), which presumes his N–S row.
- **`centerLamp` no longer gives the middle lamp the Rambam's westward face under the E–W row** (review). — "Its nozzle faces west, toward the KhK" is the Rambam's statement of his ner ma'aravi in an N–S row (BhB 3:8); placing it on lock #11's row mixed the readings. The text now says that under lock #11 the middle lamp is not the ner ma'aravi and Tamid's rite (3:9, 6:1) gives it no role of its own; only in the N–S reading is it the ner ma'aravi, with the branch lamps facing it.
- **The Ulam chains hang from the ceiling to 4 amot over the floor (+26)** ⚙. — Middot 3:8: the young kohanim "climb" them to see the crowns, so the lower end must be within reach. 4 amot (≈1.9 m) clears a walking man's head. Count, Y spacing and X are unchanged, so the two inner chains still hang before the F2 windows. — Alternative: chains to +48 with a ladder (no source has one).
- **The lul through the KhK's own wall is confirmed; the KhK stays dark.** — EK II 178–179 (with Tosafot; against Mahari): 4 tefachim, with a small curtain. Zevachim 55b: it validates the court behind the Kaporet, so it is an opening of the House. The curtain closes it, so it is not a window, and Part VII §22's "no windows — a dark chamber" holds. `walkThrough: false`. — Alternative: the lul in the outer ta wall only (Mahari). It then would not open the House.
- **Heichal gate curtain 0.1 amah (≈5 cm) off the wall face** ⚙ (X 163.1–163.27). — Its folds must not enter the wall. — Alternative: the builder's own offset only.

## Soreg
- **The breaches' side counts 4N 4S 3E 2W are labelled as borrowed** (`fieldConfidence.sideCounts: 'reconstruction'`). — Shekalim 6:3 gives them for the prostrations "opposite the thirteen gates" (with Abba Yosi b. Chanan, Middot 2:6). Middot 2:3's breaches view gives none. — Alternative: an even 13 round the soreg. That has no side counts at all, and the gates view's are the only ones in the sources.
