## Engine

- **Textures are baked on the GPU at load, per texture set, lazily (first `ctx.mats.get` of a material that needs the set); `ready()` only compiles the generator programs and builds the basis maps.** — memory and time go only to materials the world actually uses; builders call `get()` synchronously inside `buildWorld` — bake everything in `ready()` (every unused set costs memory) or ship downloaded textures (forbidden).
- **Generators are loop-free and sample CPU-made periodic basis maps (4-channel fbm, 16- and 5-cell Voronoi; half-float) instead of evaluating noise in ALU.** — measured on the HD 4600 / ANGLE-D3D11: an ALU-noise generator cost 0.3–3 s to compile + first draw *each* (fxc + driver JIT scale with inlined instruction count, and dynamic loops add a draw-time JIT); texture-driven ones cost ~0.05–0.2 s, compiled in parallel — ALU noise (≈10 s cold start on low).
- **Generators write ONE packed RGBA32UI texel (albedo sRGB8 + alpha8 · height16 + roughness8 + AO8 · metalness8) instead of an MRT.** — ANGLE's link-time pixel-shader variant covers only the first output; an MRT target forced a second synchronous compile at the first draw of every generator (60–300 ms each). With one output every set bakes in 2–16 ms including a GPU sync — MRT scratch (simpler, much slower cold start).
- **Basis maps are computed on the main thread while `renderer.compileAsync` links all programs on the GPU process's threads (KHR_parallel_shader_compile).** — the two overlap; a worker added ~1 s of dev-mode module start-up — a Web Worker (kept the CPU free but finished later).
- **Final maps per set: albedo RGBA8 sRGB, normal RG8 (three's USE_PACKED_NORMALMAP reconstructs z), ORM RG8 (AO, roughness) or RGBA8 for metal sets; mipmapped, anisotropy = `quality.anisotropy`.** — 10.7 B/texel (13.3 for metals) incl. mips; stock MeshStandard reads AO .r, roughness .g, metalness .b — RGBA everywhere (+50 % memory).
- **Normals and cavity AO come from a 16-bit height field in a shared finish pass (Sobel, 12-tap cavity), not authored per kind.** — one place to get normal strength physically right (height in meters / texel size in meters) — per-kind normal code.
- **Resolution = set's reference size × `quality.textureSize / 2048` (floored at a per-set minimum for fine weaves).** — measured with every set baked: low 18.9 MB, medium 61 MB, high/ultra 233 MB (budgets 48 / – / 256); generation on low ≈ 0.8 s (parallel compile) + 0.04 s to bake all 38 low sets — fixed sizes.
- **Masonry layouts (courses of blocks) are generated on the CPU and passed as a float lookup texture (half-quantum × row → block start, length, index, row code).** — guarantees seamless tiling, joints broken between courses (incl. the last↔first course across the vertical wrap) and block starts away from tile edges; loop-free lookup in the shader — in-shader search loops (draw-time JIT on D3D).
- **Optional shader patch (`patch.js`, chained `onBeforeCompile`, constant `customProgramCacheKey` so programs stay shared): world-space macro variation (2 fetches of the basis fbm), per-stone variation, close-range micro detail (medium+).** — breaks tiling on 200 m walls and floors at ≈1–2 ms/frame on the HD 4600 at low (8.3 ms vs 6.5 ms flat, full-screen paving); `?matfx=0` disables it, `?mattex=0` forces flat materials; skipped feature-by-feature if another patch removed the chunks it hooks — a second texture layer in every material (not stock-compatible).
- **Per-stone variation: masonry/sheet albedo alpha stores the texel's distance from its block's start; the shader recovers the block's absolute start (uv − d·dMax) and hashes it with the course index.** — every stone of a 13 m tile repeated along a wall gets its own tint/roughness at no texture cost; faded out when minified (block id unreliable) — larger tiles (memory) or visible repetition.
- **Grain runs along U; `*_v` keys (cedar_v, oak_milah_v, olive_wood_v, wood_lattice_v) bake the same wood with grain along V.** — world UVs (`applyWorldUV`) give vertical faces u = horizontal; posts and door boards need vertical grain — a UV-swap shader patch (lost if the patch is disabled).
- **`timber_rough` = bark surface for modelled logs/timbers; `timber_pile` = a stacked-log texture for the long faces of box-shaped piles (logs along U); new `timber_pile_end` = the end faces (log ends).** — a texture of many logs looked wrong on log geometry, a bark texture looked wrong on a box pile, and a side-of-logs texture on the faces where logs end read as masonry — one key for all (or choosing by face normal in the shader patch, which would be lost with `?matfx=0`).
- **`roof_tile` keeps its key but is the ma'azivah: a lime-concrete/plaster roof fill; `roof_plaster` is an alias.** — the sources give tikrah + ma'azivah courses and a parapet with the kalya orev; no roof tiles are attested (Middot 4:6; Part V §13.11) — tiles.
- **Metals: material.color = F0 (sRGB) over a near-white variation map. Pure gold (gold_plate, gold_relief, gold_vessel) uses the MEASURED F0 of gold, linear (1.0, 0.766, 0.336) → #ffe39d; greenish gold #f0e6a6, red parvayim gold #fbbd90, Corinthian bronze #f4d29e, copper #f6c6aa, silver #f6f3ec are reconstruction tints, not measurements.** — physically based; the earlier #ffd47e was more saturated than measured gold and was mislabelled "measured" — painted dark "gold" (looks like brass under PBR). Gold still reads olive in its upper half while env's environment map is sky-only (see env request).
- **Thin generated features are resolution-aware (marble veins at least ~1 texel wide; kochla thin layers, pale streaks and vein hairlines fade when narrower than a texel).** — on low (25 mm/texel for the wall) they aliased into dashed lines; at high nothing changes — separate low-tier parameter sets.

## Research reconstructions (all labelled `confidence` in `SPEC.info`)

- **Herodian wall: courses 1.1 m high, blocks 2–5 m long (0.5 m steps), joints broken ≥ 0.75 m; texture 13 × 6.6 m at 2048 × 1040 on high (6.3 mm/texel).** — sources fix the materials, not the stone sizes; within the range of excavated Herodian ashlar. A 2560 px wall texture (5 mm/texel, sharper joints at 2 m) was tried on paper but would put high at ~255 MB of the 256 MB budget — other sizes / more memory.
- **Course rhythm: colour cycles white (shayish) → blue-green (kochla) → veined (marmara) per course, while the face alternates set out / drawn in (4 cm) per course — a 6-course period. The texture bakes the soffit/shadow of each set-out course as a 6 cm darkened band (albedo −30 %, AO −45 % at the top) along the top of the drawn-in course below it, so the alternation reads at distance even where builders do not model the 4 cm (they are asked to model it at detail ≥ 1).** — Bava Batra 4a (אפיק שפה ואעיל שפה): "one course set out and one drawn in", courses of shayish/kochla/marmara; the report (Part VII §21 #1) states the three alternate; the exact sequence, the 4 cm offset and the band are reconstruction — normal-map step only (invisible past a few metres); two-colour alternation; larger offsets.
- **Dry hairline joints (1 mm half-width, 4 mm deep, 4 mm quarter-round arris, joint albedo ×0.85, AO 0.85) with small chips (more at corners); drafted margin 8.8–13.2 cm, a flat band dressed with comb-chisel strokes perpendicular to its edge (mitred at the corners), 7 % lighter and rougher; then a crisp 5 mm step up to the boss raised 8–16 mm, finely pecked with a point; honed (roughness ≈ 0.58–0.76), not polished. `herodian_wall_inner` = same coursing with plain dressed faces.** — Herodian ashlar is laid dry, with tight joints; the old 5 mm dark outlined joints and a 34 mm boss ramp read as modern "subway tile" and a picture frame. Tooling pattern and dimensions are reconstruction — polished faces; margins on inner faces.
- **Kochla: ONE palette (sets.js `KOCHLA`: base #78878a, band #65747b, streak #a9b2b0) for the herodian_wall courses and the monolithic marble_kochla; a blue-green grey stone with layered bedding — broad soft tonal zones plus thin layers along a warped coordinate elongated along the course, at modest contrast, fine dark grains and sparse pale layers parallel to the bedding (the look of the banded green marbles of the Roman world, e.g. cipollino). Marmara = white with pronounced grey veins (dark core, soft halo, crisp hairlines); shayish = white with faint, width-varying veins.** — report §26 ("blue-green"), Part X (Magen: kochla "the dark kohl-blue stone", marmara a family of hard quarry stones). The old blob mask read as camouflage and the two kochla uses did not match; veins of constant width read as cracks. Tone and banding are reconstruction, judged under the AgX "punchy" grade at d3 and sunset — saturated aqua; Magen's dark blue.
- **Har HaBayit pavement: pale weathered marble slabs in rows of 1.25 m, lengths 1–2.5 m, worn arrises, pitting, dust in hollows.** — "the whole Mount paved in marble" (Ezrat Kohanim I p.184, Part X); slab sizes reconstruction — limestone.
- **Azarah floor: "avanim yekarot" read as costly fine-dressed stone — large fitted white marble slabs (rows 1.6 m, 1.5–3.5 m long), tight 2–3 mm joints, smoother (worn by barefoot service).** — Rambam BhB 1:10 (ומרצפין את כל העזרה באבנים יקרות, verified in the Hebrew); 1 Melachim 5:17/7:9 use the phrase for costly dressed stone, not gems — gemstone mosaic.
- **Altar skin: closely packed whole rounded field-stones (~25 cm and ~11 cm) as low pillows (9 / 4 mm) with domain-warped outlines (4 cm), limited toward the cell border softly by F2−F1, combined with a smooth max, buried in broad lime fillets; thick lime whitewash wiped with a cloth (faint horizontal strokes), no trowel marks; the sikra line is red ochre painted on the same skin (same seed, so it continues the stones).** — Middot 3:4 (ומלבנים אותן פעמים בשנה... לא היו סדין אותן בכפיס של ברזל; Rebbi: מלבנים אותו במפה), Rambam BhB 2:16, Middot 3:1 (חוט של סקרא). The old version (sqrt domes clipped at the Voronoi border, combined with max) gave straight polygon edges and C1 creases; a strict "radius ≤ 0.36 cell" version read as scattered dots. Stone sizes and lime thickness are reconstruction — a flat plastered box.
- **New key `heichal_whitewash`: a thick lime wash over large dressed ashlar (courses 1.1 m, blocks 1.5–4 m), joints softened, shallow and bridged by the lime over long stretches, tooling muted, faint horizontal brush/cloth strokes, brighter than bare stone; `lime_whitewash` stays the altar's rounded-stone skin.** — Middot 3:4 (וההיכל, פעם אחת, בפסח, checked in the Hebrew), Rambam BhB 4:13, report §13.6/§13.11 ("Heichal ×1/yr (Pesach)"). Which faces were washed (the Heichal vs the bare-marble Ulam facade of BB 4a) is left to build-heichal to decide and log — no key (builders choosing between bare marble and the altar texture).
- **Beaten gold sheets 1 amah high, 1.5–3 amot long, overlapping seams, no nails; hammer dishes 1 mm deep on ~2.5 cm cells; each sheet undulating ±1–2 mm over 20–40 cm (beaten over carved wood), held flat along the seams; sheets closely matched (roughness ±0.005, tone ±0.008), and the shader patch adds no per-sheet roughness on metals.** — rikuei zahav (Shekalim 4:4), plating (Middot 4:1); the earlier per-sheet variance (±0.025 roughness, ±0.02 patch) changed how much sky each sheet mirrored and read as a checkerboard from 20 m, and 0.2 mm dishing was too weak to scatter reflections. RG8 normals kept (quantisation 0.45° is far below the ~15° lobe of roughness ≈ 0.25) — RG16F normals for metal sets (+2 MB, needs EXT_color_buffer_float).
- **Corinthian bronze (Nikanor's doors): polished cast bronze, base roughness 0.27, casting/planishing waviness ~0.8 mm over 10–50 cm, planishing dishes 0.25 mm on 3 cm, polishing streaks as roughness variation ±0.07 along U, a faint darker patina (−8 % albedo, +0.05 roughness) in the low spots; 2 × 2 m tile so the waviness noise has a single period per tile.** — Yoma 38a "gleamed like gold", unplated; the old near-mirror (0.14) with 0.06 mm relief rendered as a featureless sky gradient, and on a 1 m tile the noise basis repeated twice per tile (visible blotches every 0.5 m). Finish is reconstruction — a sky mirror; decorative relief (not attested).
- **Gold relief (Kodesh): a frieze of palm trees alternating with frontal two-winged keruvim (child-like round head) in a 1.92 m module, bands of eight-petalled open flowers above and below with bead mouldings; gold sheets seamed between motifs; relief ~1.3 cm.** — Yoma 54a (keruvim d'tzurta), 1 Melachim 6:29 (keruvim, timorot, p'turei tzitzim), Part VII §21 #20; arrangement and drawing reconstruction; avoided Yechezkel 41:18–19's two-faced keruvim — no figures (contradicts Yoma 54a) or Ezekiel's programme.
- **Parochet: every cord twisted of four plies — techelet (deep indigo-like blue #15295e), argaman (deep red-purple #3b0f36), tola'at shani (kermes crimson #6e0c19), shesh (natural linen #aaa089) — 12 mm cords in a 2/2 twill (floats two cords long), 24 mm twist so each float shows all four plies (the colours mix evenly and the tile does not show), deep shadowed gaps; the finer linen thread takes ~12 % of the surface, varying gently across the cloth; no woven figures; dark sheen tint. Far mix on screen ≈ sRGB (109, 74, 79), a deep heathered red-violet (was a pastel (163, 136, 135)).** — Shekalim 8:5 (פרכת עביה טפח... על שבעים ושתים נימין... עשרים וארבעה חוטין, checked in the Hebrew: a tefach thick, 24-thread cords = 4 materials × 6), Yoma 71b (ma'aseh chosheiv); the tefach thickness itself is geometry for build-heichal. The cord scale, weave, twist, dye tones and linen share are reconstruction; the Second-Temple sources attest no figures (Rashi's lion/eagle is a general illustration of chosheiv) — keruvim (First-Temple/Mishkan), separate colour stripes, 4 mm cords (invisible past 1 m), equal linen share (reads pastel pink).
- **Shesh linen ~16 threads/cm, six-ply twist visible up close; curtain linen ~10/cm.** — six-ply per the report (Part VIII §34); thread counts reconstruction.
- **Firewood: fig (smooth warm grey bark, ring creases, blotches), walnut (dark grey-brown, interlacing furrows 9 mm deep), oil-tree read as Aleppo pine (grey-brown plates ~1 cm proud over dark red-brown fissures); never olive or vine. Pile: one round log per block, radius varying 12–16 cm, deep dark gaps (albedo ×0.16, AO 0.15) between logs, 15 % split logs showing pale wood; pile ends (`timber_pile_end`): log ends with off-centre pith, growth rings, bark rim, radial drying checks, some split billets, muted seasoned colours.** — Tamid 2:3 (מרביות של תאנה ושל אגוז ושל עץ שמן, verified in the Hebrew); etz shemen = pine is the common identification, not stated in the Mishnah; the old pale fig bark and shallow gaps read as a brick wall — other species.
- **Cedar reddish-brown fine grain, milah (oak/ash) with ray flecks, olive yellow-brown with dark figure, soreg slats weathered grey-brown; soreg screen panel = slats ~4.5 cm crossed at ±45°, ~17 cm apart.** — Middot 2:3 with Bartenura (diagonally crossed slats), Middot 3:7 (milah), Part VII §26 (browns); slat sizes reconstruction.
- **Iron = black oxide with hammer marks and rust where weathered; copper (kiyor) = used copper/bronze with oxide tarnish and a little verdigris; Corinthian bronze polished, warm gold sheen.** — Part VII §24; Yoma 38a (Nikanor's doors "gleamed like gold") — bright untarnished copper.
- **Even Shetiyah = natural limestone bedrock (rough, fissured, unworked); terrain = weathered grey Jerusalem limestone with karren and lichen; terra rossa soil with limestone pebbles.** — Yoma 5:2/53b; regional geology (reconstruction) — worked stone.
