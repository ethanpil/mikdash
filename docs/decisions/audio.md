# audio — decisions

Format: **choice** — why — alternative. Everything not fixed by the sources is reconstruction and flagged as such.

## Engine (src/audio)

- **All sounds are synthesised in pure-JS DSP (`src/audio/synth/`) inside two module Workers, then cached as AudioBuffers; the live Web Audio graph only plays, spatialises, reverberates and mixes.** — no frame hitches on weak devices; the same code runs in node for tests; sample-level algorithms (Karplus-Strong strings above 375 Hz, modal banks, formant cascades with per-period jitter, stick-slip friction) are impossible or awkward as Web Audio node graphs (a feedback loop needs ≥ 128-sample delay) — OfflineAudioContext node graphs built on the main thread.
- **Two render lanes: "heavy" (choir, crowd babble) and "light" (everything else).** — a 2–4 s choir render once delayed a trumpet past its cut-off in testing; now signals render in ~0.1–1 s regardless — one queue with priorities (a running job cannot be pre-empted).
- **Synthesis at a fixed 48 kHz (32 kHz on low); AudioBufferSourceNode resamples. Impulse responses are rendered at the context rate.** — buffers rendered before the AudioContext exists (pre-warm on medium+) stay valid on any device; ConvolverNode requires a matching rate — render everything at the context rate (no pre-warm before unlock).
- **The AudioContext is created only inside `unlock()` (UI gesture, `audio:unlock`, or our own first-gesture listener); Safari's `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'`.** — creating it earlier logs Chrome's autoplay warning; the session type keeps iOS' silent switch from muting the Temple — create at init and resume later.
- **Loudness model: each source has an estimated SPL at its near-field radius r0; the mix maps dBFS = −10 + 0.6·(SPL − 100); distance uses the PannerNode's exponential model with rolloff 0.6.** — real levels span ~40 dB (night insects) to ~118 dB (the magrefah) and do not fit a screen; one global compression keeps their order and ratios, and lets two silver trumpets (≈112 dB at 1 m) stand ~16 dB above the crowd 200 m away, "heard across the Mount" — inverse 1/d law with hand-set gains (tested: a trumpet at 200 m only matched the pre-dawn bed).
- **SPL estimates** (dB at r0): trumpets 112@1 m, magrefah 116@1, cymbal 110@1, Gevini 106@1, Barkai 104@1, great gate 101@3, gate boom 100@2, chalil 94@1, choir 89@3 (12 men + boys + strings), muchni 90@2, shalom-call 90@1, grinder 88@1, sheep 88@1, knock 84@1, nevel 80@1, kinnor 78@1, pyre 76@4, crowd murmur 62@10 per 300 people (+10·log10 N/300), bells 72@1, keys 72@1, doves 70@1, footsteps (barefoot) 56@1, torch 56@0.5, water 56@1, night insects 48, doves bed 47, wind 43. The aggadic ranges ("heard in Yericho", Tamid 3:8) are expressed as the loudest entries, not literally — literal 10-parsa audibility.
- **Reverb: one ConvolverNode per acoustic space of the listener (open, mount, stoa, court, hall, chamber, heichal, tight), cross-faded on zone change; procedural stereo IRs (three-band RT60, sized early reflections, flutter in narrow passages, tone filter).** RT60 low/mid/high: open 0.9/0.7/0.45 s · mount 2.0/1.7/1.1 · stoa 2.1/1.8/1.25 · court 1.7/1.45/1.0 · hall 2.7/2.3/1.6 · chamber 1.2/1.0/0.7 · **Heichal 5.2/4.7/3.7, bright (gold-plated, 40×20×40 amot)** · tight 1.1/0.9/0.6 — acoustic estimates for the geometry and materials of report Part VII (reconstruction) — one global reverb; an algorithmic FDN reverb (cheaper CPU, less natural).
- **Reverb send by a critical-distance model** (rc: open 40 m, mount 18, court 14, stoa 7, hall 3, Heichal 2, chamber 1.6, tight 1.3; reverberant level falls beyond rc with α = 1 open … 0 enclosed), in the same compressed scale; a source in another space couples at 0.6 (0.3 into an enclosed room). — inside the Heichal the room dominates beyond ~2 m; in an open court the direct sound dominates to ~14 m — fixed per-sound wet levels.
- **Air absorption lowpass fc = 22 kHz / (1 + d/65 m); occlusion by `ctx.physics.raycast` (2/4/6 rays per 100 ms on medium/high/ultra, none on low): −8 dB and a darker lowpass.** — distance must darken as well as soften across a 250 m Mount; walls between source and ear muffle — no occlusion (the Heichal's walls would be transparent).
- **Plus a raycast-free transmission floor on every tier: when source and listener are in different acoustic spaces, occlusion is at least 1 − (1 − T_listener)(1 − T_source), T = Heichal 0.9, chamber 0.8, hall 0.75, tight 0.6, stoa 0.3, court 0.3, Mount/open 0.** Area and moving sources re-locate their space every tick (an area source is located at its nearest point to the listener); a source starts at its floor, never open for the first ticks. — the low tier has no rays and area sources never had any, so a court trumpet, the crowd, the pyre and the sheep reached the gold room unmuffled; rays only refine occlusion within one space — rays on every tier (CPU on phones; no help for area sources).
- **Area sources may be several boxes** (`area: [box, …]`, nearest box wins). — the crowd stands on courts at different floor heights (Ezrat Nashim +6, Ezrat Yisrael +13.5) and the amah has two open stretches — one bounding box (wrong heights; sound from the middle of nowhere).
- **HRTF panning on high/ultra, equalpower otherwise; voice budget 14/24/36/48 incl. active loops; quietest voice is stolen (priority-weighted), a stolen loop waits 3 s.** — the task's tiering; HRTF costs a convolution per voice.
- **Loops are virtualised: inaudible ones (< −60 dBFS at the ear) release their nodes and resume at their "virtual playhead".** — dozens of torches and fires cost nothing until the observer is near — always-on loops.
- **Delayed cues fix their start on the audio clock but claim a voice slot only 150 ms before they sound.** — the libation sequence (≈95 s of scheduled cues) stole its own first psalm section in testing — claim at schedule time.
- **Captions (`caption` bus event) are emitted even while sound is locked, if the call would be audible (≳ −48 dBFS); while sound runs the same audibility test is made when the call actually sounds.** — the moment (Gevini, Barkai) is not lost for a viewer with sound off; distant faint calls do not spam subtitles. By the loudness model a Barkai or Gevini (104–106 dB) is still captioned 400 m away; a "Shalom alecha" (90 dB) is not.
- **A time jump cancels every one-shot: playing, scheduled, and still rendering in the Worker; and any libation in progress.** — §9: cues sound only when time passes them during continuous play; a choir whose render finished after the jump used to start seconds later.
- **Cue normalisation measures the loudest 400 ms K-weighted (ITU-R BS.1770: +4 dB shelf above ~1.7 kHz, 38 Hz highpass), not flat RMS; sparse loops likewise; steady beds keep long-term flat RMS −20 dBFS.** — flat RMS counted the gates' 30–80 Hz energy as loud and left them far quieter than their level estimates; K-weighting leaves voices within 0.1 dB, lowers trumpets 1.5 dB and bells 3.7 dB (they were too loud for their energy) — an A-weighted measure (over-penalises the low end at these levels). The beds were tuned by live metering and stay as they were.
- **One-shot variants: a random variant, but one already rendered if the drawn one is not; the drawn one renders in the background.** Medium and up pre-render every variant of the common cues. — the first trumpet or magrefah of the day must not wait seconds on a phone — always variant 0 (no variety).
- **Render cache keys include the tier for the tier-dependent renders (choir, crowd); a quality change drops buffers the new tier never asks for (other synthesis rate, other IR length) and rebuilds the current room's reverb.** — singer/talker counts differ per tier; old pinned beds stayed in memory.
- **Outdoor beds (wind, insects, doves) share a bus muffled per space (hall 0.22 / 1.1 kHz, Heichal 0.1 / 650 Hz …).** — stepping into the Moked or the Heichal must close out the night — per-bed occlusion.
- **Impulse responses capped by the tier (low 2.2 s) keep a natural decay: if the cap would cut the tail above −40 dB, all three RT60s shrink in proportion (on low only the Heichal: 5.2/4.7/3.7 → 3.3/3.0/2.3 s, still the longest, brightest room).** — a 5 s tail cut at −24 dB with a 0.3 s fade sounded gated, in the one room meant to ring — a longer IR on phones (convolution cost).
- **What is pre-rendered when (Worker time).** Medium and up: beds, every variant of the common cues and the day's three psalm sections at unlock, low priority. Low (phones): only the beds the ambience is using, the sounds of the next three scheduled events, and — whenever a scheduled event with the Levites' song starts within the next ~5 real minutes (sim minutes scaled by the clock speed) — the libation set (blasts, cymbal, prostration, three sections). With no schedule data yet, low fetches the song once at idle priority a minute after unlock. — unlock used to start ~12 s of single-core work on every tier (3× that on a phone) for a libation hours away.
- **Glottal-source wavetables are built by one inverse FFT per table (identical to 1e-14) instead of a direct cosine sum.** — each Worker builds them once; the direct sum cost ~1 s before the first voice.
- **Any user gesture re-unlocks sound while the context is not running (a passive capture listener, kept for the whole session).** — iOS/Safari leave the context 'interrupted'/'suspended' after a call or app switch and only resume it inside a gesture; the old listener was removed after the first unlock.

## The music (report §27.1, §29, §54; Arachin 2:3–6; Tamid 7:3–4)

- **Melodies are not preserved: a modal reconstruction.** Unison psalmody on a reciting tone: intonation F–G → recitation on A → mediant cadence B♭–A(–G) at the half-verse → termination G–F–E–D at the verse end, in a mode on D with B♭, Pythagorean tuning (D3 = 146.8 Hz). — reciting-tone psalmody with two cadences follows the two-part structure of the biblical verse itself and is the common shape of the oldest surviving Jewish (Yemenite, Babylonian) and early church psalmody; Pythagorean ratios are the pre-Renaissance lyre tuning. Reconstruction, not a claim about the actual Levitical melodies — later synagogue modes (e.g. the augmented-second "Ahava Rabbah"), Western harmony.
- **Texture: the 12+ men in unison; boy Levites an octave above with simpler lines (Arachin 13b, "sweetening the treble"); 9 kinnorot (lyres) plucking the melody heterophonically; 2 nevalim (larger, lower) sounding the final and fifth; strings ~10 dB under the voices.** — "the essence of the song is the voice" (Sukkah 50b–51a, §29.2) — instrumental accompaniment as an equal partner.
- **Every tier renders at least the attested minimum on the duchan: 12 men, 9 kinnorot, 2 nevalim (ultra 14 men). Boy Levites (number not attested): low 2, medium 3, high 4, ultra 6.** Weak tiers save per-voice cost instead: low synthesises at 32 kHz with a 32-sample control rate and without the parallel high-formant branch, medium a 24-sample control rate. — the render is a one-off Worker job whose result costs the same to play, so cutting performers saved nothing at run time; measured here: 12+2 voices on low ≈ 1.1 s per section (less than the old 5+2) — fewer singers on weak devices (below the attested minimum).
- **The cue sings the opening verses of each third of the psalm of the day (Tamid 7:4: Sun 24 … Shabbat 92).** — the Mishnah fixes three sections, not where they fall; full sections would be minutes of audio per render — the full psalm text.
- **Divine names are rendered in their customary substitute forms (Hashem, Elokim, Kel).** — a simulation, not liturgy.
- **The libation concert (`ctx.audio.libation`): three teki'ah-teru'ah-teki'ah sets, each with the people's prostration, each preceding a section; Ben Arza's cymbal at the pour cues the first section.** — Sukkah 5:5 counts nine blasts for the tamid; Tamid 7:3 has the first set before the pour and a teki'ah + prostration "at each section" — reading the nine as three sets that open the three sections is one coherent count; the alternative reading (sets after each section plus the opening set = 12) contradicts the nine.
- **Where the blasts sound from: the opening set from the table of the fats; then "they came and stood beside Ben Arza, one on his right and one on his left" (Tamid 7:3), so the two later sets sound from beside him on the duchan (≈ 28 m away). Their walk sets the pause before the pour (distance at 1.4 m/s, 4–25 s; ≈ 20 s on the site model; `opts.walk` overrides). One centred source for the pair (1.5 amot either side of Ben Arza is inaudible as a spread).** — the Mishnah's own staging — all three from the table.
- **The people also prostrate at the opening set.** — a consequence of the nine-blast reading together with "and at each teki'ah, a prostration" (Tamid 7:3) — prostrating only at the breaks between sections ("they reached a section, blew, and the people prostrated"), which would leave the opening set without one.
- **Teki'ah ≈ 3.1 s, teru'ah = nine short tongued blasts in three groups (≈2.5 s); two trumpeters in unison, a few cents apart (audible beating).** — the halachic norm that a teki'ah and a teru'ah are of similar length; the exact timing is reconstruction.
- **Trumpet pitch A4 (≈440 Hz), the choir's reciting tone; bright, brassy additive tone whose upper partials grow with lip pressure, small-bell radiation (little low end).** — Chazal give no trumpet length (§64, Menachot 28a: hammered block silver); a short straight trumpet sounding one held partial; tuning it to the reciting tone keeps the blasts and the song in one tonal world — reconstruction.
- **Ben Arza's cymbal: two bronze plates clashed (Radak, per §54): dense inharmonic modes 380 Hz–11 kHz, crash noise, high partials decaying first.** — Shekalim 5:1 (Ben Arza over the tziltzal); one instrument only (Arachin 2:5).
- **Kinnor = lyre (plectrum, small soundbox), nevel = larger and lower (finger, deeper body) in the default layer.** — Mishnaic usage; SG's harp/lute identifications belong to the style layer — harp/lute timbres by default.
- **In the `sg1612` layer the strings follow SG's identifications (report §54): kinnor = arpa (harp — finger pluck near mid-string, long ring, soft contact, a long soundbox with broad low resonances), nevel = liuto (lute — paired courses a few cents apart, plucked near the bridge, ribbed-bowl body, strumming bass-tenor-alto of the falsobordone chord); 12 players = 9 harps + 3 lutes.** — Magen I gives "12 singers + 12 players" without the split; 9 + 3 keeps Arachin's minimum of 9 kinnorot and stays within its 2–6 nevalim — the lyre/nevel timbres in both layers (the setting promises "the 1612 reading of the instruments").
- **The chalil (a reed pipe, double-reed character) is a cue but never enters the weekday song; `fluteDay: true` adds it.** — Arachin 2:3: only on the 12 flute days.
- **No organ-like sound anywhere.** — the hydraulis was not used in the Mikdash (Arachin 10b).
- **Style layer `sg1612` (setting `musicStyle` or `opts.style`): four-voice homophonic psalmody (falsobordone) — bass, tenor (the psalm tone), alto, cantus — in quarter-comma meantone with cadential musica ficta (C♯, F♯) and a Picardy final; sections 1–2 by a group of four, section 3 by all twelve; the cymbal at every break; men only.** — Shiltei HaGiborim (Portaleone, Mantua 1612), report §54: "12 singers… three groups of four, each singing its section in four voices… all twelve in the last"; falsobordone and meantone are the psalm practice and tuning of SG's own Mantua. A Renaissance lens, never the default — flagged.

## The cues (sources in the report §28–29, §36.3)

- **Barkai (Tamid 3:2):** a lookout's held shout "Bar-kai!"; variant `hebron` = Matya ben Shmuel's version ("Is the whole east lit, as far as Hebron?" — "Hein!"). Caption in both languages.
- **Gevini (Yoma 20b; Shekalim 5:1):** "Imdu kohanim la'avodatchem, uLeviim l'duchanchem, v'Yisrael l'ma'amadchem!" as a crier's chant — three phrases on a high reciting pitch, each ending on a long carried note (a crier's projection, "heard three parsaot").
- **Shalom-call:** the Ish Har HaBayit's "Shalom alecha!" to each post (Middot 1:2); variant `all-well` = the two torch companies meeting, "Shalom! Hakol shalom." (Tamid 1:3).
- **Muchni:** Tamid 1:2 names the sound "the wood Ben Katin made" → a wooden wheel's groaning axle, frame creaks and peg knocks in four hauls, with water streaming off the rising kiyor.
- **Great gate (Tamid 3:7):** two locks, the bar (negar) drawn, two pairs of leaves groaning on their pivots, the boom at the stops — the slaughter's signal.
- **Door booms (gate-boom, great gate) are built for small speakers too: low leaf modes 31–118 Hz, wood-plate partials 148–715 Hz of comparable weight, a knock transient around 400–1200 Hz, and an asymmetric soft clip of the low layer whose 2nd/3rd harmonics (highpassed at 150 Hz) carry the boom's pitch; the groan's resonator bank no longer favours 38–92 Hz.** — a phone or laptop speaker rolls off below ~250 Hz: the old booms lost 19 dB there and vanished into the bed; now 7 dB — a sub-only boom (realistic on full-range speakers only).
- **Magrefah (Tamid 5:6):** a heavy metal shovel hurled on stone: impact, bounces, a ringing blade, a slide; it also hushes the crowd murmur for the ketoret (§36.3), released a few seconds after the prostration.
- **Bells:** 36 distinct small soft-gold bells (of the 72 or 36, Zevachim 88b), damped between pomegranates, a jingle cluster per stride — "audible before visible".
- **Grinder (Keritot 6b):** "Hadek heitev, heitev hadek" as a work-chant in time with the pestle in a stone mortar.
- **Footsteps:** barefoot on stone (Zevachim 24a) — heel thump, skin slap, toe roll; no shoe clicks anywhere.
- **Keys:** chain drawn through the hand, the great keys knocking (Tamid 3:6 — one reaching the armpit), set down on the marble slab (Middot 1:9).
- **Crowd prostrate (Tamid 7:3):** a wave of cloth, knees and palms on stone, one long exhale — silent on a weekday (spoken responses belong to Yom Kippur, §33.5).
- **Formant voices:** Klatt-style cascade (F1–F5 from the standard sung-vowel tables) + a parallel high-formant branch, band-limited glottal-pulse source with jitter/shimmer, vibrato, drift, portamento, consonants from transliterated Hebrew; ancient pronunciation is unknown — a neutral Sephardi-like vowel set.

## Ambience director (src/audio/ambience.js)

- **Automatic population: ~60 kohanim and Levites from before alot; the courts fill to ~320 and the plaza to ~110 from sunrise (the gates open with three blasts); emptying after sunset.** — §36.5 "baseline ma'amad + staff ≈ low hundreds"; the plaza is empty before the gates open — a constant crowd. Festivals: `ambience.setPopulation(n)`.
- **Two court murmurs: the staff (kohanim and Levites on duty — men only, sparser babble) over the Azarah floor and the Beit HaMoked; the public (35 % women's voices) over Ezrat Nashim and Ezrat Yisrael, each at its own floor height, only once the gates are open.** — before dawn only the kohanim and Levites are inside, and they are in the Azarah and the Moked, not the outer courts — one mixed crowd in the outer courts at all hours (and placed below the Ezrat Yisrael floor). Whether the Azarah or only Ezrat Kohanim should carry the staff is a judgement: the kohanim work across the whole court.
- **The magrefah's hush is released only by a prostration that comes after that hush began (≥ 1 s), 4 s after it; a time jump releases any hush.** — the first prostration of the day used to cancel every later hush within 0.5 s.
- **Fires follow `ctx.fx.list()` live: positions every 0.5 s (a carried torch moves with its bearer), and a fire that leaves the list ends its loop.**
- **The amah is the site model's own course (`azarah.amah-channel` open stretches: past the altar's SW corner and the kevesh foot), as a two-box area source.** — replaces my earlier reconstruction (shitin → Water Gate).
- **Sheep in the chamber of the tamid lambs (Moked, Middot 1:6; ≥ 6 lambs, Arachin 2:5); doves as a bed of rock doves (nesting in the walls) and turtledoves; crickets and the Mediterranean tree cricket at night.** — reconstruction of the natural soundscape of a Jerusalem autumn; the kinim's own position is for the sim to cue.
- **Night insects follow the season and the night's temperature (reconstruction): calling activity full April–October, 0.35 in November, 0.3 in March, none December–February, silent below ~10–12 °C; temperature = the month's mean minimum for Jerusalem (interpolated by day) + 4 °C early in the night falling to the minimum by dawn; chirp and trill rates scale with it by Dolbear's law (N ∝ 7.2·T − 32), the bed's power with the rate. Default date (2 Nov, before dawn ≈ 14 °C): sparse, slow chirps about 12 dB under a summer night (activity −9 dB, fewer chirps −3 dB).** — the old bed chirped at a warm-summer rate every night of the year — season only (no temperature).
- **Only the nearest 1/2/3 torches (low/medium/high+) are voiced; oil lamps and the menorah are silent.**
