# time-env — decisions

Format: **choice** — why — alternative.

## Time (src/time)

- **Israel Standard Time (UTC+2) all year, no DST** — the contract; the service follows the sun, not a civil clock, so one fixed offset keeps minutes comparable across the year — Israel civil time with IDT (+3) in summer (every summer time would shift by an hour).
- **The clock runs on one fixed civil date and wraps 24:00 → 00:00 of the same date** — "a day in the life" loops seamlessly and the sim stays a pure function of (date, t); only `setDate()` changes the date — advance the date at midnight (the zmanim, moon and Hebrew date would jump during play).
- **Night seasonal hour = (1440 − day length)/12** (the complement of that date's sunrise→sunset) — evening and pre-dawn hours join seamlessly across the wrap; differs from the true sunset→next-sunrise night by < 1 min — the true night length (a visible discontinuity at the wrap).
- **chatzot = sunrise + 6 seasonal hours (GRA midpoint); midnight = sunset + 6 night hours** — consistent with toZmani/fromZmani (day hour 6 is exactly chatzot); within seconds of solar transit / ~1 min of solar midnight — solar transit from the equation of time.
- **Zmanim at sea-level angles** (alot −16.1°, sunrise/sunset −0.833°, tzeit −8.5°) — the contract and the standard Jerusalem calendars. The *visible* sunrise over Har HaMishchah (a ridge ~4° high from the Mount) is ~20 min later; the sky and lighting show that real sunrise (terrain occlusion), the zmanim do not — elevation-adjusted sunrise.
- **Barkai = alot (−16.1°)**: Tamid 3:2 / Yoma 28b "the whole face of the east is lit up to Hebron"; the Gemara contrasts the moon's glow that "rises like a staff" with the dawn that "spreads this way and that" (Yoma 28b, the doe's branching horns of Tehillim 22:1). The dawn glow is therefore a horizontal band spreading along the eastern horizon and a sideways-spreading glow that persists where the sun will rise, not a column — a column-like dawn.
- **Hebrew date from `Intl` (calendar 'hebrew')**, Hebrew numerals generated locally (ICU 'he' prints Arabic digits); `hebrewAt(t)` switches at sunset (the halachic day begins at night) — a hand-written calendar.
- **dayType from the weekday only** (Fri = 'friday', Sat = 'shabbat') — the contract; festivals later.
- **Sky computed for the chosen modern date** (2026 sun/moon/planets, J2000 stars, no precession to the 1st century) — consistent with the 2026 zmanim the user sees; ~30 CE would shift the stars ~27° along the ecliptic and needs the Julian calendar — an "ancient sky" mode.
- **Moon: Meeus ch. 47 truncated + topocentric parallax** (~0.05°; parallax is up to 1° and matters) — the 7-term almanac series (~0.3°, the Moon's own diameter).
- **Planets added** (JPL Keplerian elements, 1800–2050) — not requested, but on the default date the half Moon stands ~66° up with Jupiter (−2.0) and Mars beside it before dawn, and Venus (−4.3, 15° from the Sun) rises shortly before sunrise; leaving them out would be a visible inaccuracy.
- Verified: 2026-11-02 → 22 Cheshvan 5787, alot 04:42, sunrise 05:56, chatzot 11:22, sunset 16:49, tzeit 17:26 IST; full Moon 2026-10-26, last quarter 11-01, new Moon 11-09 reproduced to the hour; Jupiter/Saturn oppositions 2026-01-10/10-04 to < 3°.

## Environment (src/env)

- **Hillaire-2020-style LUT atmosphere**: transmittance (256×64) and multiple-scattering (32×32) LUTs once; a sky-view LUT (128×64 … 320×160) re-rendered only when the Sun/Moon move (≥0.01°), lit by BOTH Sun and Moon; the dome does one lookup per pixel — cheap enough for the HD 4600 (idle 0.1 ms/frame, 0.2–1 ms per LUT update) and correct from noon to deep twilight — a per-pixel ray-marched sky (too slow on low) or Preetham/Hosek (no twilight, no night).
- **Aerosol: AOD(550) 0.14 at sea level, scale height 1.6 km, Ångström 1.0, g 0.76** — Eastern-Mediterranean autumn (continental + desert dust); gives the soft whitish horizon, golden hours and visible haze on distant hills — Bruneton's demo default (AOD ≈ 0.005, an alpine sky far too clean for Jerusalem).
- **Ozone red channel uses the ~600 nm Chappuis cross-section (2.1e-3/km), not 680 nm** — the sRGB red primary samples the band's peak; with the 680 nm value the twilight zenith came out magenta (r/g 1.24) while measured twilight zenith chromaticity (x≈y≈0.22) gives r/g≈0.45 — the textbook (0.65, 1.88, 0.085)e-3.
- **The view ray is split at the Earth's shadow cylinder** in the sky-view march — the lit/shadowed boundary falls on a segment edge, removing the twilight "rings" coarse steps produce — more steps (still aliased, slower).
- **Photometric units with pre-exposure**: sun in lux, sky in cd/m², everything multiplied by `ctx.env.preExposure` (= key/La) so the frame buffer holds ~0.01–100 from starlight to noon, inside half-float range (sun glints on gold would overflow to Inf at physical scale; night would underflow at a fixed scale). `ctx.env.exposure` (≈1) is what the tone mapper applies. Fires/lamps/emissive must multiply physical values by `preExposure` — physical units with exposure only at tone mapping (contract-literal, breaks HDR range).
- **Absolute levels checked against measurements**: noon (Sun 43°) diffuse 14 500 lx, beam 98 000 lx, zenith 2 280 cd/m²; sunset 770 lx; Sun −3° 130 lx; civil dusk (−6°) 5.4 lx; −10.5° 0.03 lx; starlight 0.001 lx; half-Moon beam 0.019 lx — all within measured ranges.
- **Exposure curve** (key vs log La): 0.18 by day → 0.13 at sunset → 0.065 at civil dusk → 0.03 half-moon → 0.018 starlight, adapting with τ 0.7 s and snapping on time jumps; Purkinje tint on moonlight/ambient below ~0.1 cd/m² — night reads as night but legible — full photographic auto-exposure (night looks like day).
- **One shadow-casting key DirectionalLight: the Sun by day, the Moon below Sun −4°** (`moonLight` is the same object) — moonlight is < 0.1% of skylight whenever the Sun is above −4°; one light means no second per-fragment light and no shader recompiles when roles change — two directional lights.
- **Shadow camera: ±80 m around the camera (snapped to texels), growing to ±220 m and 2.6 km deep toward a low Sun** — at sunrise Har HaMishchah (when built with castShadow) shadows the whole Mount until the Sun clears the ridge, and tall façades catch first light first ("the east face catches first light", §28; nivreshet at sunrise, Yoma 37b). Verified with a test ridge 70 m high 1 km east: shaded at sunrise+8/+16, a 30 m tower's top lit first, all lit at +30. `setShadowsManaged(false)` hands the light to the render task's CSM; then (and on low, which has no shadows) the key light is dimmed by a terrain horizon profile seen from the camera (`setHorizonProfile`, or sampled from `ctx.physics.floorAt` 350 m–4 km out on 'ready').
- **PMREM from the sky only** (no sun/moon disc — the directional light gives the highlight), with the sunlit local pavement (albedo ≈0.48, Jerusalem limestone/marble) below the horizon; stored normalised by sky irradiance and scaled every frame by `scene.environmentIntensity`, so ambient changes continuously between refreshes (refresh at ≥0.25–1° of Sun motion, halved in twilight; 32/64/128 px by tier) — a HemisphereLight (no directional sky colour for gold/marble) or refreshing every frame.
- **Aerial perspective by replacing three's fog chunks**: fog applied in linear HDR *before* tone mapping (stock three mixes fog after tone mapping, so tiers disagreed), physical extinction (Rayleigh rgb + aerosol with 1.6 km scale height) along the real view ray, in-scattered colour = horizon sky radiance around the dominant light (2nd-order Fourier fit measured from the LUT). Extra uniforms are uploaded straight into every compiled program that declares them; until then (a program's first frame) they are zero = no fog (≈1 % at 200 m, invisible), never a stock-fog flash. `scene.fog` (FogExp2) stays set for USE_FOG and for stock-fog consumers (FX) with density √(σ/300 m) ≈ 4.8e-4, so exp2 equals the physical extinction at Temple distances (see time-env-fix.md). The same override clamps HDR output to 6e4 (half-float safe) — per-material `onBeforeCompile` (would fight other tasks' materials) or depth-based post fog (needs the post pipeline; low has none).
- **Stars: the ~210 real bright stars (V < 3.7, J2000) + procedural faint stars to V 5.6/6.2/6.5/6.8 by tier, denser toward the galactic plane**; brightness is perceptual — contrast over the sky behind each star from the naked-eye limiting magnitude for that sky luminance (fit: 6.5 at 2e-4 cd/m², 4.3 moonlit, ~0.5 in civil twilight, −4 by day). Stars fade in at dusk as for the eye and Venus/Jupiter show in twilight although a display pixel is ~4′ — flux-correct sprites (Venus invisible in twilight; a camera, not an eye).
- **Moon disc with Lommel–Seeliger shading from the real Sun direction** (phase and terminator orientation follow automatically), maria at their selenographic positions, earthshine; the disc is compressed to ~3 output units (local adaptation) so the phase reads instead of a white blob — physical radiance (always clipped at night).
- **Low Sun disc locally adapted**: one factor brings the disc centre to max(3 output units → unlimited by 25°, 2–5 × the sky beside it), a log shoulder above that keeps the brighter upper limb; refraction flattens it (Bennett gradient, ≈0.78 on the horizon) — the setting Sun keeps its colour, stays the brightest thing in view, and is blinding at noon (see time-env-fix.md).
- **Night-sky floor 2e-4 cd/m² at the zenith (≈21.8 mag/arcsec²)**: airglow van Rhijn-brightened and seen through the air, plus that light scattered by the horizontal air path (×2 zenith) — 2.3× zenith at 10°, 1.7× at the horizon, continuous into the aerial haze below it; the same curve feeds stats, fog, ambient and star visibility (see time-env-fix.md) — a dark ancient sky (lamps of the city negligible) — modern suburban sky.
- **Cirrus (high/ultra only)**: thin fibrous deck at 8.5 km, lit through the atmosphere (pink after sunset/before sunrise); coverage is a deterministic property of the date (45% of dates clear, else 0–0.5) or `ctx.env.cirrus` / `?cirrus=` — random per session (non-deterministic) or none.
- **Milky Way (high/ultra only)**: procedural band in galactic coordinates (brighter toward Sagittarius, Great Rift dark lane), rotating with the stars — a texture (no downloaded assets allowed).
- **The narrow solar aureole** (coarse dust, HG g 0.99) is analytic because the LUT cannot hold a 1–3° peak; it uses the disc's own line-of-sight transmittance and adaptation factor, so it vanishes with the Sun below the horizon. The pre-sunrise glow that "spreads this way and that" is the sky-view LUT's own twilight band (superseded: an earlier sub-horizon aureole drew a false Sun — see time-env-fix.md).
