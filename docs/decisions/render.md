# render — decision log

`- **Choice** — why (source / reasoning) — alternative.`

## Pipeline
- **Tiers: low = direct render (renderer tone mapping, FX as a second depth-tested draw); medium = HDR composer → FX → bloom + grade → SMAA; high = + N8AO half-res; ultra = + N8AO full-res "High", god rays, vignette.** — the task's tier spec; low must stay fluid on an HD 4600 (measured 4.3 ms/frame on the test plaza) — a single pipeline with passes toggled would cost the HalfFloat composer even on low.
- **postprocessing + n8ao are lazy-loaded (`render/post.js`, dynamic import) only for post tiers.** — the low tier (phones, iGPUs) skips ~330 kB (160 kB gz) it never uses — static import (one chunk).
- **FX are drawn after AO by a custom `FXPass`, depth-tested in the shader against the composer's stable depth texture (soft intersections, occlusion), GL depth test off.** — AO must never darken a flame; soft particles need scene depth anyway — FX in the main scene (AO over flames, hard intersections; N8AO's transparency mode would also re-draw them).
- **N8AO `transparencyAware = false` set explicitly.** — its auto-detection traverses the whole scene every frame and turns on a second transparent draw — leave auto (CPU cost grows with the world).
- **Tone mapping: AgX with a mild "punchy" look (CDL power 1.08, saturation 1.35 in AgX log space), one curve for both paths (`CustomToneMapping` chunk + `MikdashGradeEffect`).** — compared AgX / ACES / Neutral on firelight, sunlit white marble and gold (shots `tm-*`): ACES gives the most vivid fire and gold but rolls white marble to near-clip (sRGB 0.89 at 1.0 vs 0.80 here) and skews hues at dawn; plain AgX keeps marble gradation but drains gold and turns fire peach; Neutral shifts flame bases toward pink. The look restores saturation while keeping AgX's highlight handling and hue stability for the dawn scenes that drive this project — ACES (`?tm=aces`), AgX (`?tm=agx`), Neutral (`?tm=neutral`) remain selectable.
- **Exposure parity gains (`TM_GAIN`: agxl 1.15, agx 0.99, neutral 1.41, aces 1.0).** — env tuned its exposure against three's ACES; gains solved numerically so 18 % grey lands on sRGB 0.50 with every operator — re-tune env per operator.
- **Bloom threshold expressed in display terms (`threshold / exposure`, 1.3), mipmap blur, half res.** — only true highlights (flames, sun glints on gold, the sun) bloom at any time of day; sunlit marble never does — fixed scene-linear threshold (blooms everything at night).
- **Bloom prefilter (render-fix): the SKY never feeds bloom (scene depth at the far plane => 0) except a 0.6° disc around the key light (sun by day, moon by night); energy ABOVE the threshold with a quadratic soft knee (±0.6 × threshold); output clamped to 4 × threshold; bloom blended ADD.** — postprocessing's smoothstep mask passes whole pixels once over the threshold, so the bright sky round the sun formed an iso-luminance contour that the box-filtered mips turned into a 25° pink ellipse at dawn/dusk and a rounded-square blob at noon (review: medium/high/ultra). env's analytic aureole already carries the sky glare. SCREEN blending is `dst + src − min(dst·src, 1)`, which darkens HDR pixels ≥ 1 — exactly the flames it should brighten. Verified: sunset / sunrise / noon on medium and ultra now match the low tier's crisp sun + aureole (`shots/render-fix/b1-*`, `std-sunrise-medium`) — a separate sun-glare sprite (one more draw, duplicates env's aureole).
- **Sun shafts (ultra, render-fix) replace postprocessing's GodRaysEffect: added light = key-light colour (40 % desaturated) × phase(angle of the PIXEL to the sun; lobes 4° and 20°) × V, where V = decay-weighted fraction of the screen path toward the sun that is OPEN — sky, or a horizontal surface below eye level (normal from depth derivatives). Half res, 64 jittered samples, ADD; strength ramps with sun altitude (full below ~10°, 40 % at 45°, off at night); the pass is disabled when the sun is off-screen.** — GodRaysEffect depth-tested its disc (at 0.85 × far) against the copied scene depth and rejected it: the ray mask was empty (A/B max 2/255) while still costing a copy, a light pass, 64 samples and a blur; its SCREEN blend darkens HDR ≥ 1. A sky-brightness blur makes the horizon a false occluder (a hard shelf of light across every column at horizon height) and treating the ground as a source smears the aureole into a blob below the sun (both tried: `shots/render-fix/gr3-on-ultra`, `gr7-sunrise-ultra`). With V, walls and columns carve shadow streaks and the gaps carry beams (A/B mean 8/255, max 74/255 through the test colonnade, `gr8-cols-ultra`); an unoccluded low sun gets only a mild symmetric haze glow. Crepuscular rays at temple scale are physically subtle (tens of metres of shadowed air against kilometres of in-scatter), so the strength stays restrained — the stock effect with depthTest off (still a sky-brightness blur, with the shelf).
- **SMAA in its own final pass (after tone mapping) with dithering.** — edge detection on display-referred values; SMAA cannot merge with other convolution effects — SMAA inside the grade pass (edges detected on HDR).
- **Renderer info counts the whole frame (`info.autoReset = false`, reset at frame start).** — the shot tool and `ctx.post.stats` otherwise report only the last fullscreen pass.
- **Benign ANGLE/HLSL compiler warnings (X3595, X3557, X3570, X3571, X4000, X4121, X4122) are filtered from three's Program Info Log via `setConsoleFunction`; everything else passes through.** — they come from library shaders (N8AO sampling loops) and env's LUT shaders, are not actionable and drown real warnings — disable `checkShaderErrors` (hides real errors).

## Shadows
- **Cascaded sun shadows use three r186's native `SunLight` (2-cascade atlas inside WebGLRenderer) with a subclassed `CascadedSunShadow` that sets an explicit split.** — `examples/jsm/csm/CSM.js` globally replaces `ShaderChunk.lights_fragment_begin` with an older copy lacking r186's sun-light/probe sections and overwrites every material's `onBeforeCompile` (clobbering the materials task's hooks); the stock SunLightShadow "practical" split puts the cascade boundary ~F/4 out (≈100 m), wasting near texels (≈4.5 cm texels to 32 m now) — CSM.js with 3–4 cascades.
- **Mirror env's key light (sun by day, moon by night) into the SunLight each frame and hide env's light only for the duration of the draw; env's own `castShadow` is turned off while render owns shadows and restored on tier change; `env.setShadowsManaged(false)`.** — env keeps sole ownership of colour/intensity/direction; light counts stay constant (no recompiles) — ask env to build its key light as a SunLight.
- **Cascade ranges (render-fix): high split 24 m / far 210 m at 2048² per cascade (texels ≈ 3.1 cm / 27 cm); ultra 30 m / 300 m at 4096² (≈ 1.9 cm / 19 cm); in fly mode `far` grows 3 m per metre of camera height above 12 m (max 700 / 900 m). `quality.cascades` (3 on high, 4 on ultra) is not used: r186's SunLight has exactly 2.** — the old 360 / 520 m ranges gave cascade 1 ≈ 0.45 m texels (≈ 1 m blur with PCF) over the Azarah (65 × 90 m), where most architecture is seen; beyond `far` env's terrain-visibility dimming takes over (env uses it when render manages the shadows). Texel sizes are bounding-sphere fits for 16:9, 62°; live cascade widths match (63.5 / 549 m high, 79 / 785 m ultra). Ultra's 8192 × 4096 depth atlas is ≈ 128 MB — acceptable for the GPUs ultra targets — CSM.js with 3–4 cascades (see above), or 3072² on ultra (≈ 25 cm texels).
- **PCF radius 2.2 (high) / 2.6 (ult.) texels, bias −2e-4, normal bias 0.03–0.035 m.** — r186 PCF = 5 Vogel-disk hardware-PCF taps; at ~4 cm texels this gives a ~10 cm near penumbra (crisp-but-soft), and the far cascade's larger texels naturally soften distant wall shadows (a 56 m wall's real penumbra at its far end is ~0.5 m: 0.53° sun) — PCSS (needs raw-depth blocker search; impossible on the same compare-mode depth texture).
- **`renderer.shadowMap.type = PCFShadowMap`.** — r186 removed PCFSoftShadowMap (warns and falls back) — leave engine's setting.
- **Safety net: force one shadow render for any shadow-casting key light whose map was never created.** — env sets `shadow.autoUpdate=false` while the key light is off; starting at night leaves the light's `sampler2DShadow` bound to a non-depth texture, Chrome rejects every lit draw (`GL_INVALID_OPERATION: Mismatch between texture format and sampler type`) and all geometry vanishes (found while verifying night shots); render forces one shadow render (after deciding the frame's autoUpdate) — reported to env.

## Adaptive resolution
- **Scale the drawing buffer 0.5–1.0 × the tier's pixel ratio in 0.1 steps: down after 1.5 s below 90 % of target, up after 4 s at the vsync ceiling, 2 s cooldown, a scale that fails within 5 s becomes a 30 s ceiling; frames > 250 ms ignored; off in shot mode.** — holds ~55 fps desktop / 30 mobile without thrashing (ultra on the HD 4600: 86 ms → 40 ms at 0.6 within 14 s, still converging) — continuous scale (constant re-allocation), dynamic tier switching (recompiles).

## FX
- **Fires are physical: flame luminance (cd/m²), coal bed / ember luminance, light intensity (cd ≈ flame area × luminance), inverse-square decay, all × `env.preExposure` (env contract).** — pyre 1.5e4 cd / flame core ≈ 2.2e4 cd/m²; torch 300 cd; oil lamp 3 cd; hearth 2.5e3 cd; brazier 1.2e3 cd. A daytime pyre therefore reads as a pale ordinary fire, a night pyre lights the court — artistic constants (looked fine until env became photometric; then overflowed half-float at night).
- **Local eye adaptation to firelight: fire light and emission are scaled by a = (La / (La + La_fire))^0.85, La_fire = 0.6·0.3·E_fire(camera)/π, eased over 0.8 s.** — env meters only sun/moon/sky; physical fire values × a starlit preExposure (~100) overflow half-float and blow out; adapting only the fire terms behaves like human local adaptation (fire and moonlit surroundings both legible) — have env add `ctx.fx.illuminanceAt(camera)` to its meter (global adaptation; then call `ctx.post.setFireAdaptation(false)`).
- **Emission soft cap (luminance-preserving): 3 display units on post tiers, 0.75 on low (low tone-maps every layer separately); render-fix lowered both (were 6 / 2.2).** — keeps flame cores pale yellow and bodies orange instead of clipping to white, and bounds bloom energy — no cap (white blobs).
- **Fire-adapted emission gain (render-fix): `uFireGain = min(1, 1.8 × cap / (1.25 × 2e4 cd/m² × P))` multiplies all fire emission (flames, coal beds, embers, glare).** — looking into a fire the eye adapts to the flames: at night a flame is ~100 × over the cap, so the soft cap flattened core, body and tips into one cream mass. The gain maps a reference flame core to 1.8 × cap and keeps relative luminance (core 1.25 L, body ≈ 0.5 L, coals 0.1 L), so the flame keeps its structure; by day the gain is 1 (flames already in range) — only a lower cap (tried: still flat, `f3-n4-high`).
- **Flame body brighter and yellower (render-fix): emission ∝ (0.35 + 0.9·heat²) (was 0.12 + heat²), ramp 0.22–0.55 → (1, 0.47, 0.07).** — a luminous wood-flame body is ~1–3e4 cd/m²; the old body (≈ 0.12–0.4 L) sat at clear-sky luminance (4–8e3 cd/m²), so the altar fire read as faint mauve ribbons by day (review) — keep the old emission and only change blending (still dim).
- **Flames are premultiplied (One, OneMinusSrcAlpha) with alpha = soot emissivity of the tongue: ε = 1 − exp(−1.3 m⁻¹ × width), × edge coverage — a 1 m pyre tongue ≈ 0.7, a torch ≈ 0.2, an oil-lamp flame ≈ 0.04.** — Beer–Lambert soot absorption (κ ≈ 1 m⁻¹ for luminous wood flames): a big sooty flame hides the sky behind it, so the sky can no longer tint it pink, and overlapping tongues converge to the flame colour instead of summing to white (low tier). Day flame pixels are now R > G > B with median B/R ≈ 0.38 (medium 162/101/65, high 158/95/59; were B ≥ G) — a fixed alpha 0.3–0.5 (tried 0.4: pale peach through the grey smoke).
- **Low-tier glows (halo, embers, coal bed) use screen blending (One, OneMinusSrcColor); post tiers stay additive.** — on low every layer reaches the 8-bit canvas already tone-mapped; screen saturates toward the colour instead of clipping to white. In an HDR buffer OneMinusSrcColor goes negative above 1, so post tiers must stay additive — additive everywhere (white mass on low).
- **Low-tier flame edge widened (alpha smoothstep over 0.45 of the shape distance; post 0.3).** — low renders at 0.8 scale and upscales; the narrow edge stair-stepped at the tongue tips.
- **Glare halo ≤ 0.6 × size for pyre / hearth / brazier (torch 1.2, lamp 0.5), purely exponential to exactly zero at the sprite edge, no rim (render-fix).** — the 1.1 × size pyre halo read as a 4.4 m aura disc around the altar fire on low: the supernatural glow the Second Temple did NOT have (Part VIII §32; Yoma 21b); physiological glare is small — no halo (low has no bloom: fires look pasted on).
- **Low-tier smoke: 24 puffs (was 16) at 0.8 density, gentler noise erosion (0.7 vs 1.1) for its single octave.** — 16 puffs stretched along a 46 m column plus one fully weighted noise octave tore the column into ghostly strips (review) — one scrolling-noise column billboard (a different look from the other tiers).
- **Fire light colour ~1900 K partly white-balanced (pyre 0xff9d55, torch 0xffa862); flame colours stay fully saturated in the shader ramp.** — the eye adapts chromatically to firelight; fully saturated orange light turned whole courts orange — raw blackbody colour.
- **Pyre = Second-Temple ordinary wood fire: orange soot flames, grey wood smoke, coal bed, embers; NO glow, colour shift or smokelessness.** — report Part VIII §31–§32; Yoma 21b (the heavenly fire "crouched like a dog" and did not assist; smokeless/sun-clear are First-Temple properties); woods Tamid 2:3.
- **Smoke columns never bend: puffs churn internally but the column axis is vertical; no wind input at all.** — Avot 5:5 (wind never prevailed over the column of smoke); incense smoke narrower and whiter, "rises like a stick" (Yoma 38a, verified in the Hebrew: מתמר ועולה כמקל) — a wind-driven plume (contradicts the sources).
- **Smoke is lit physically: env key light (sun or moon) with wrap-diffuse + Henyey-Greenstein forward scattering, env ambient, and the fire beneath by inverse square of its physical intensity.** — silver-lined columns at dawn, orange under-lit columns at night, from the same data as every other surface — artistic glow gradient.
- **Four-to-five instanced draw calls for ALL fires (flames, coal beds, halos, embers, smoke), analytic GPU particles, a 128² runtime noise texture (4 Perlin octaves in RGBA) instead of ALU noise.** — HD 4600: FX cost 0.3–1.6 ms on the test plaza; no downloaded assets — per-fire meshes / CPU particles.
- **Real lights: a constant pool of `quality.maxFireLights` PointLights; fires within 1.6 m merge into one light (the menorah = one light); clusters ranked by intensity/d² with 1.35× hysteresis; weights ease in/out and a slot is handed over only after its owner faded.** — constant light count = no shader recompiles; no popping — adding/removing lights (recompiles every material).
- **Fire shadows (render-fix): fixed shadow-casting slots in the light pool from boot — high 1, ultra 2; only STATIC pyre / hearth / brazier clusters take them; a second ranked sub-pool holds everything else (a cluster moving between pools cross-fades). Cube maps render on assignment or move and refresh every 0.25 s (high) / 0.125 s (ultra, staggered) for moving casters; shadow lights never jitter; near 0.3 m.**
- **Fire shadows are deliberately SOFT: small cube faces (high 128², ultra 160²) with PCF radius 3 / 4 (≈ 0.025 rad of blur), bias mostly a world-space normal offset (10–12 cm, depth bias −1.5e-4).** — a 2 m fire is a large source: penumbra ≈ size × (receiver − blocker) / blocker distance, i.e. metres at the altar's own edge and ~0.5 m for a column 30 m away; 512² faces gave razor-sharp CG edges (`final-alot-high`), and a wide kernel on a large face showed the 5-tap Vogel pattern as halftone grain (`soft-alot-*`); taps ≤ 2 texels apart on a small face stay smooth (`soft2-*`). A depth bias grows with distance² on a cube map (6e-4 ≈ 1.8 m at 30 m) and detaches distant shadows — PCSS (impossible on compare-mode depth), large faces. — at night the altar fire on its 10-amot altar is the dominant light: the altar body must shadow the Azarah floor, columns and steps must cast (review: the whole court was lit uniformly, solids floated; now `fire-n4`/`fs-colonnade-n4-*`). Constant light and shadow counts = no recompiles; a static world makes the maps cheap between refreshes. Medium has none (its frame budget is spent; at night its single shadow is env's moon). Cost driver: StaticBatcher merges each material over the whole site, so every refresh draws the full world 6 × (no per-face culling) — see report issues — per-frame cube updates (6 × world every frame).
- **`ctx.addFire`'s default `size: 1` (core/context.js) is read as "unspecified" for lamps and torches (refSize < 0.5 m): they get the preset size (lamp 0.08 m / 3 cd, torch 0.35 m / 300 cd).** — a 1 m oil-lamp flame is never intended; otherwise a menorah registered without sizes becomes seven 1 m flames merged into ≈ 3.3e3 cd instead of 21 cd. Explicit sizes are honoured; pyres / hearths / braziers keep size 1 (plausible). Requested: drop the default in core/context.js — leave it (silent integration trap).
- **Extended fires place their light at 0.6×size above the base.** — the centre of the flames; avoids a point-light hot spot on the surface the fire stands on.
- **Carried torches (`attachTorch`) lean against their motion and are never merged with static fires.** — flames trail a moving torch (Ish Har HaBayit's patrol, the inspection companies' torches, Tamid 1:3).
- **Schedules: `night` fades in between sun altitude +3° and −3°; `always`/`service` burn at the spec intensity until `setFire`.** — the sim owns service timing (e.g. menorah lamps, Simchat Beit HaShoevah) — hard-coded service hours in render.
- **FX fog: FX use their own fog (transmittance only for additive emitters, full blend for smoke) with `fog:false`.** — env's aerial-perspective chunk adds in-scattered sky light inside `tonemapping_fragment`, which would add fog light onto additive flames and needs a varying FX don't compute.

## Device tiering
- **`detectTier(renderer)` = GPU-string classification refined by a ~10–70 ms fragment-ALU micro-benchmark in a separate tiny WebGL2 context, cached per GPU string; bench may move the string's tier by at most one; mobile capped at medium.** — HD 4600 scores 5.9 → low; bounds 15 / 48 / 180 ≈ 2.5× / 8× / 30× that iGPU (Iris Xe/M1 → medium, GTX 1060 → high, RTX 3060+ → ultra); a separate context keeps three's GL state cache untouched — string-only (misses laptop variants, unknown GPUs).
