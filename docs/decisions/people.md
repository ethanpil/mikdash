# people — decisions

Format: **Choice** — why (source / reasoning) — alternative.

## Research-driven (costume, bodies, poses)

- **Kohanim wear exactly four white linen garments, barefoot** — report §34.1 (Yoma 7:5; Rambam KhM 8), barefoot: Zevachim 24a (nothing between foot and floor). Garments modelled new, fitted and unhitched (ketonet to above the heel with sleeves to the wrist). — n/a (sourced).
- **Michnasayim not modelled** — they run hip-to-knee and are fully covered by the ketonet in every pose we show; modelling them would only cost triangles. — add a short tube if a pose ever exposes the legs (e.g. a climbing pose).
- **Ketonet "meshubetzet" drawn as a ~8.5 mm boxed weave in the fragment shader: softly raised boxes set off by shallow sunken lines** — §34.1 "boxed meshubetzet weave"; procedural so it costs no textures. Every periodic pattern (box weave, avnet windings and kilayim figures, techelet twill, hair, cords, basketry, the face's fine lines) fades to its *average* by Nyquist: fully detailed while a period spans >= 4 px, fully averaged at <= 2 px (`P_NY(P)`), so nothing aliases into moire or crawls when the camera moves (the first version faded too late and showed a graph-paper grid and false stripes). — a woven normal map texture (forbidden: no downloaded assets) or geometry.
- **Avnet wound high, at the lower chest ("over the heart"), ~8 cm band of many windings** — §34.1 (32 amot × 3 fingers, wound round and round over the heart); kilayim per Rambam shown subtly as small techelet/argaman/shani wool figures on linen; Yom Kippur avnet pure white linen. No long hanging ends (not in the research). — Rebbi's/R' Elazar's alternative (all linen) for the hedyot.
- **Migba'at = rounded dome ("like a hat"), mitznefet = lower, broader wound turban** — Rambam KhM 8 (hedyot winds it like a hat, KG like a bandage); 16-amah winding shown as a helical ridge. — identical shapes for both.
- **Tzitz low on the forehead with hair showing between it and the mitznefet** — Zevachim 19a (the KG's hair was visible between tzitz and mitznefet); gold band ear to ear, techelet cord tied behind (Shemot 28:36–37). — tzitz pinned onto the turban.
- **Tzitz inscription rendered as "קדש לה׳" (canvas texture at runtime)** — the Name is abbreviated, as is customary when the Name is printed; at real viewing distance it reads only as raised lettering. — the full Tetragrammaton (avoided out of respect).
- **Me'il entirely techelet, sleeveless, to the feet; 72 gold bells + 72 wool pomegranates alternating** — §34.2 (Zevachim 88b; Rambam KhM 9:3, 72 ⚠️ or 36: we follow 72). Ketonet sleeves show from the me'il's armholes; the avnet is hidden under the me'il (dressing order). LOD1 shows the hem as a fringe of alternating tabs; LOD2 a gold band. — 36 bells.
- **Ephod as a back apron from the lower chest to near the heels, cheshev belt, shoulder straps with the two shoham stones in gold settings** — §34.2 + Rashi's description (apron behind, to the heels); colours: shoham = very dark onyx (Bamidbar Rabbah 2:7 "very black"). — Rambam's slightly different extents.
- **Ephod/choshen/cheshev weave = a plain weave of ~1 mm composite threads, EACH twisted from 28 strands: 6 techelet, 6 argaman, 6 tola'at shani, 6 shesh and 4 of beaten gold** — report §34.2 "gold thread spun with techelet/argaman/tola'at-shani/linen (28 strands)" (Yoma 72a; Rashi Shemot 28:6: the gold beaten into threads and twisted with each kind). Because every thread carries all five materials, the cloth is ONE even colour, a dusty rose-mauve with a gold sparkle (3/14 of each dye + 2/14 gold, metalness about 0.24), and the strands show only within arm's reach. The first version drew separate 2 mm single-dye bands, which is not what the sources describe and aliased into false orange/white/blue stripes. — banded single-colour threads, or a solid-gold look (what most illustrations show).
- **Choshen stones are oval cabochons with a flattened table, standing ~8 mm proud in low gold settings (mishbetzot), 5.4 x 4 cm in a 3 x 4 grid of the zeret (half an amah = AMAH/2, so it follows ?amah=)** — Shemot 28:16-20. (The first version rotated the radii so the stones stuck out edge-first like coins.) — flat inlaid stones.
- **Stone order: Hebrew order as read by someone FACING the Kohen Gadol: each row from the viewer's right (the wearer's left, -x) to the viewer's left; odem top-right** — the names are engraved to be read, and the common traditional depictions read the rows like a Hebrew text from the front. — the wearer's right-to-left (odem on his right, the viewer's left); there are opinions both ways.
- **Choshen stones and colours: four rows of three; colours after the tribal banners of Bamidbar Rabbah 2:7** (odem red, pitdah green, bareket white-grey, nofech sky-blue, sapir deep blue, yahalom white, leshem blue, shevo wine-amber, achlamah grey (black-white mix), tarshish sea-green, shoham black, yashfeh multicoloured/purple) — a traditional Jewish identification that maps every stone. — Targum/Rashi/Rabbeinu Bachya gem identifications (several conflict).
- **Kohen Gadol blessing pose 'raise_hands_low' (hands at the forehead, not above the Tzitz) is used automatically for kohen_gadol / kohen_gadol_white** — stam Sotah 7:6, report lock #37. — R' Yehudah (above the Tzitz).
- **Levites: plain light (undyed wool/linen) long tunic with sleeves to the wrist, cloth belt, optional mantle with tzitzit; bare-headed** — §34.2: no Mishnaic uniform (II DH 5:12's white butz is First-Temple); kept distinct from priestly white by warm beige tones. **Reconstruction.** — white linen (would be confused with kohanim).
- **Israelites: undyed/natural wool tunic with two darker clavi stripes (madder/brown), cloth belt, rectangular mantle open in front with tzitzit at 4 corners (one strand techelet), optional head-cloth** — tzitzit with techelet per Bamidbar 15:38; clavi stripes and mantle colours follow Judean textile finds of the period (reconstruction, kept modest). — plain tunics.
- **Everyone is barefoot on Har HaBayit** — Rambam BhB 7:2 (no entering with one's shoes; checked in sources/Mishneh Torah) / Berachot 9:5. — n/a.
- **Women: long dress, belt, mantle, and a veil covering the hair, framing the face, falling over shoulders and back** — task + modesty norms of the period (head covering). Veils in light natural/blue-grey/madder tones so they read as cloth, not hair. — darker veils.
- **Elders/Sanhedrin: older rig (slight stoop), long grey-white beards, mantles; Sanhedrin always with a head-cloth (sudar)** — task; the sudar as scholars' head covering (reconstruction). — no head-cloth.
- **Nazir: unshorn hair to the shoulders and a long beard** — Bamidbar 6:5. — n/a.
- **Statures: men 1.67 m, women 1.55 m, elders 1.64 m (stoop), Levite boy (~12) 1.45 m, child (~7) 1.18 m; per-figure ±3–5 %** — skeletal-remains estimates for the period cluster around these values (reconstruction). — taller, modern statures.
- **Skin olive to light-brown (randomised per figure), dark hair, greying by role** — task's population brief. — n/a.
- **Full-length prostration (hishtachavayah): kneel → hands to the floor → flat, face down, arms and legs spread** — Shevuot 16b / report §35.2; phase 0–0.3 down, 0.3–0.7 flat, 0.7–1 up, so a crowd prostrating at each teki'ah (Tamid 7:3) is one phase curve. Crowd members move almost together (±2.5 % phase jitter). — kidah (bow) only.
- **Birkat Kohanim: hands raised ABOVE the head, palms forward, fingers spread** — Tamid 7:2 / Sotah 38a. — shoulder-height hands (outside the Mikdash).
- **Silver trumpets raised, at the lips, both hands on the tube; length 0.55 m** — Tamid 7:3 (trumpeters at the fats-table); Chazal give no length (report §65.6) → modest one-amah-ish instrument. — longer trumpets of later iconography.
- **Kinnor with seven strings, nevel with ten** — Arachin 13b (the kinnor of the Mikdash had seven strings); "nevel asor" (Tehillim 33:2). Shapes after the lyres on Judean coins of the period (box lyre / round-bodied lyre). **Reconstruction.** — harps.
- **Ben Arza's cymbal = a pair of bronze plates, clash at phase 0.5** — Tamid 7:3, Shekalim 5:1. — single cymbal struck with a stick.
- **The gizrin (logs) = split slabs 1 x 1 amah (AMAH, follows ?amah=), thin as a se'ah strickle** — report §65.3 (Zevachim 62b). — round logs.
- **Three coal pans: `machtah_silver` (daily scooping pan, 4 kav, silver), `machtah_gold` (daily: gold 3 kav, heavy, thick-walled, short handle, GREENISH gold) and `machtah_gold_yk` (Yom Kippur: gold 3 kav, light, thin, long handle "so that his arm helps him", with a ne'ashtik, RED parvayim gold)** — Mishnah Yoma 4:4 and Yoma 44b (checked in sources/Yoma), report §65.2 table, §34.3. Capacities sized from the kav (24 eggs, about 1.38 l at R' Chaim Naeh's measures) scaled with the chosen amah. The ne'ashtik is drawn as a ring at the handle's end; the word's sense is uncertain (reconstruction). Gold tints read `gold_green` / `gold_red` from ctx.mats when the materials task provides them. — one gold pan for both days (the first version's YK pan was used for the daily ketoret).
- **Meat props are shaped from anatomy: the hind leg (regel) as haunch, hock and shank with a marbled cut face and the femur's round; the flayed carcass with rib relief, spine ridge, the alyah fat tail and jointed legs ending in bone; outer surfaces glossy fat-and-fell with red muscle showing through where thin, cut faces dark marbled muscle (#8a3a2e)** — six kohanim carry the limbs in every tamid (Tamid 4:3), so they are hero props. — ellipsoids with spots (first version).
- **Mizrakot have no flat bottom (they lie tilted when set down free)** — Pesachim 5:5. — flat-based bowls.
- **The tamid lamb has the broad fat tail of the local breed** — the alyah is offered with the right hind leg (Tamid 4:3); Awassi-type fat-tailed sheep. — thin-tailed sheep.
- **Animals at LOD0: a lofted head (poll, brow, face, muzzle) with eye sockets, glossy dark eyes, nostrils, a mouth line and a jaw weighted to the jaw bone (it chews when grazing); legs as one jointed tube from deep inside the barrel (forearm/gaskin, knee or point of hock, slim cannon, fetlock, sloping pastern) on two-claw cloven hooves with dewclaws; the sheep in clumped fleece down past the knee with short white hair below; the goat black with long drooping Levantine ears; the bull with dewlap and a low hump** — the animals are central to the daily scenes. Breeds are reconstruction (local fat-tailed sheep, black goats, red-brown cattle). — the first version's cylinder legs and ball heads.
- **'bound' lamb pose: on its side, legs gathered (held "hand and foot", not tied)** — Tamid 4:1 (akedah). — legs tied.
- **Kohanim never sleep (or lie down) in the holy garments: any kohanic role in 'sleep' / 'lie' switches to its own clothes (the Israelite archetype: tunic, mantle with tzitzit, head bare) and shows the four garments folded under its head on its own wool mat (kesset); role `kohen_offduty` = a kohen in his own clothes** — Tamid 1:1 (checked in sources/Mishnah Tamid): they did not sleep in the holy garments but took them off, folded them and laid them under their heads, and covered themselves with their own clothes; each one's kesset on the ground. Report §34.1 / Part XIII. The folded bundle is placed by a 'pillow' grip class computed from the lying pose's head position. — a warning only (the sim would have to remember), or garments beside the head.
- **'sit' assumes a seat ≈ 1 amah high, origin = floor point under the seat** — no sitting in the Azarah except kings (report §35.3): the sim decides where sitting is allowed (e.g. Gazit, Moked ledges, Ezrat Nashim). — floor sitting.
- **No faces with modelled eyes: eyes, brows, lips and nostrils are drawn in the fragment shader from face coordinates** (dim off-white sclera, dark iris, lash line), with a sculpted nose/brow/cheek/chin — crisp at any tessellation and never "uncanny" at walk-through distances. — modelled eyeballs.

- **Arms hang just clear of the hips: the rig's 9° rest splay is taken back per body type so the wrist clears hips + tunic + forearm (about 2-6° out), elbows soft (10°), fingers relaxed (30°)** — the first version held every figure in an A-pose with the hands 10-15 cm off the thighs. — a fixed adduction (women's wider hips would clip).
- **Necks and shoulders: the neck flares into the trapezius at its root and is fuller at the nape; the shoulder line falls about 5.5 cm from the neck root to the shoulder tip on a curve (steep near the neck); the collar and trapezius rings rise toward the back. Hair reaches the nape and veils/head-cloths close over the back of the head** (an acos clamp bug had cut every hair cap and veil crown at the head's equator) — the mannequin look came largely from a straight neck on a flat shoulder plate and a bowl-cut hairline.
- **Beards are volumes, not decals: thickening from nothing at the cheek line to about 1.3 cm, broken into soft locks, closed underneath back to the throat. LOD0 hands: a rounded palm block with a thenar pad, four separate two-segment fingers with knuckles and nails, a three-part thumb lying along the index, and a wrist** — close-range dignity; LOD1-3 keep mittens.

## Engineering

- **Every figure (hero actor or crowd member) is an instance in a GPU-skinned InstancedMesh batch (archetype × LOD); skinning matrices come from one baked half-float animation atlas** — one draw call per batch for any number of figures, CPU cost ≈ 40 floats per figure per frame, identical look for heroes and crowds. — three.js SkinnedMesh per actor (one call per actor, CPU skeleton per actor) for heroes + a separate impostor crowd system.
- **Rigid rest frames (identity rest rotations) and procedural pose functions baked per rig** — authoring poses as functions of phase (with analytic two-bone IK for planted feet and fist placement) keeps every pose deterministic and sim-drivable; bake ≈ 50–150 ms per rig, lazily when a rig is first used. — keyframed clips.
- **Atlas layout: frames stacked in blocks of 1024 rows side by side (96 texels = 32 bones × 3 per frame)** — the texture never exceeds 1024 rows (WebGL2 guarantees only 2048) and addressing is two integer ops. Half-float (≈ 0.5 MB per human rig). — float32 (2× memory).
- **Cross-fades are blended in LOCAL space (slerp per bone) on the CPU into 64 scratch atlas rows** (texture `updateRanges`, one row per actor per frame) — linear blending of skinning matrices visibly shrinks limbs mid-transition (seen and fixed). Crowds switch poses instantly (one-shots start from the standing pose anyway). — dual-quaternion blending in the shader.
- **Garment variety by per-instance palettes (skin, hair/grey, garment A/B, stripe) and optional garment parts collapsed by a per-instance mask** (mantle, head-cloth) — variety without extra draw calls. — one archetype per variation.
- **Props are skinned to one bone per instance (hand / chest / clavicle) with the attach point in the instance data** — props follow the animation for free; grip classes fist / tray / shoulder / free; left-hand variants are mirrored geometry. — CPU-parented prop meshes.
- **LOD by projected height: LOD0 hero (~7–9k tris, fingers, sculpted face; KG with all 144 bells ≈ 20k), LOD1 (~1.4–2k, mitten hands), LOD2 (~0.5–0.65k, 2 bone influences, no frame interpolation, no shadows); hero count capped per tier; quality.maxFigures enforced nearest-first (actors before crowd members)** — keeps the HD 4600 'low' tier at ~55 fps with 60 figures and medium at 35–45 fps with 160–180 figures in the debug scene. — billboard impostors (would need a pose × view atlas; LOD2 meshes are already near impostor cost and keep animation).
- **Cloth detail is a derivative bump (Mikkelsen perturbNormalArb) on a procedural height — no tangents, no textures; sheen (MeshPhysicalMaterial) only on detail ≥ 2** — cheap on iGPUs, rich on strong GPUs. — normal-map textures.
- **Floors: `setSite(X, Y)` and crowd members without Z stand on the WALKING floor (physics.standFloor: the standable floors of the column, preferring the site's datums Mount 0 / EN 6 / EY 13.5 / Azarah 16 / Heichal 22, the lowest of those), never on the roof, beam or column top above; `setSite(X, Y, { near: Z })`, `points: [{ pos, near }]` and `region.near` pick the floor nearest a height (stairs, galleries, upper rooms); when physics is not ready figures keep a provisional height and are re-resolved whenever the colliders are (re)built (physics.version)** — the first version asked physics for the topmost surface and put figures under the test plaza's architrave on the beam (y = 10.3 m). A per-zone floorZ hint was rejected: stacked zones (roof over the Heichal, the Ulam's upper storey) make a 2D zone lookup ambiguous. — zone-based heights.
- **Torch flames follow every bearer every frame (drawn, culled or behind the camera) from the baked hand bone; flames of bearers beyond the far cap go out (intensity 0); a free torch (spawnProp) stands upright and burns** — the first version placed anchors only for drawn figures (flames left at the site origin or frozen). — CPU skeletons.
- **Beyond quality.maxFigures the next figures (up to 3 x maxFigures) are drawn as LOD3 silhouettes (about 250-360 tris, 2 bone influences, no frame interpolation, no shadows, no props except big ones); within the budget, figures smaller than about 14-30 px also use LOD3. The last 20 % of the far allowance dithers out (4 x 4 ordered dither on LOD1-3) instead of popping; the shadow casters of figures dithered more than half out collapse** — on low (maxFigures 60) a 131-figure scene now draws all 125 in view at 55.8 fps vs a 55.9 fps empty baseline (HD 4600); the first version dropped 71 of them and they popped at the boundary. — impostor billboards (would need a pose x view atlas).
- **Region crowds in a walking pose flow along their headings at the stride-matched speed (driven by the auto phase or by the sim's explicit phases) and wrap inside the region, dithering in/out within 1.6 m of its edges; their floor is re-resolved round-robin; points crowds walk in place (warned once: moving people should be Actors)** — the first version's crowds moonwalked. — warn only.
- **Crowd `roles` accept animal kinds (a flock: `roles: 'lamb'`); unknown roles are warned and skipped (not silently made Israelites); per-point headings (`points: [{ pos, heading }]`) win at creation and `setHeadings()` without an argument restores them** — reviewer findings.
- **Crowd `layout: 'prostrate'` (automatic when created prostrating) gives each member a 2.1 : 1 cell along the facing axis, staggered row to row, with little jitter** — a full-length hishtachavayah needs about 2.3 x 1.1 m; square cells made neighbours' hands and feet overlap. How much room to show is the sim's choice: Avot 5:5's "standing crowded, prostrating with room" is a miracle. — expanding positions during the go-down (figures would slide).
- **InstancedMesh batches are disposed (instanceMatrix and per-instance buffers) when they grow, on quality change and on dispose** — a GPU buffer leak otherwise.
- **people clamps the frame dt to [0, 0.25] s** — three's Timer gives a negative first delta against rAF timestamps (measured −1.39 s in the harness), which rewound cross-fades. — trust the engine dt.
- **Stand-alone dev harness `src/people/dev/` (harness.html + shot.mjs + probe.mjs; probe-main.mjs runs checks in the main app)** — lets people/ be developed and screenshot while other subsystems are mid-edit (site.js was broken for a while during this task). Not part of the production bundle (only index.html is an entry). — test only inside the main app.
